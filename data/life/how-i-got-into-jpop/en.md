The main reason I wanted to write this post was to share my [J-Pop concert attendance list](https://www.parkerchang.life/live). One of the nice things about having a personal website is being able to make little corners exactly the way I like them!

I originally wanted to write about my history with music, but since it's already the last day of the month, I'll share how I got into J-Pop for this gathering and save the rest for another time.

My earliest memory of J-Pop is probably the first Digimon series' theme song, [Butter-Fly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YAxNwRb93xk).

Then, sometime in middle school, a friend got me into anime. Listening to music wasn't as convenient back then: I downloaded songs onto my computer and copied them to an MP3 player. The first few songs I probably saved myself were the opening theme from _Angel Beats!_, [My Soul, Your Beats!](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Lia+My+Soul+Your+Beats), its ending theme, [Brave Song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aMreXisyr3U), and its best-known insert song, [一番の宝物 (Yui final Ver.)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=%E4%B8%80%E7%95%AA%E3%81%AE%E5%AE%9D%E7%89%A9+Yui+final+Ver).

After that, I started saving songs from all the other anime I watched, mostly the popular anime themes of the time: _One Piece_, _Bleach_, _Naruto_, _Gintama_, and so on.

But from the time I joined the dance club in high school until I left the street dance scene after graduating from university, I was constantly listening to Western hip-hop and funk tracks so I could practice. That was actually a pretty stifling period for me. I listened to music with a very specific goal in mind, always thinking about how I could dance to each song instead of really feeling it. Part of that was because, when I was young, I cared too much about dance battles. But a bigger part was that I wasn't interested in those songs, so all I had left was analysis, without much feeling.

I couldn't really listen to J-Pop while dancing, either, because those songs rarely came on at competitions. Once I stopped dancing, I felt liberated: I no longer had to think about whether a song was suitable for dancing. And since I had always watched anime, I started listening to a lot of J-Pop again.

One song that gave me a lot of strength in those earlier years was Mika Nakashima's [僕が死のうと思ったのは](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QL3T2Nzcqcs) (The Reason I Thought I'd Die). The link is to a 2016 live performance, and it's truly overwhelming. It gives me the drive and resolve to keep going. I still pull it up every now and then, and I'm so glad I got to hear it live in Taiwan in 2025!

For a while, J-Pop music videos were so beautifully made that I'd put one on a screen while I coded. I used to watch Zutomayo, Eve, Yorushika, Yama, Minami, and others.

Up to that point, most of the J-Pop I listened to was closely connected to anime. But the main reason I like J-Pop is that it's easy to find bands, and the sound tends to be light and energetic (even if the lyrics are often another story).

I really fell into J-Pop after COVID restrictions were lifted at the end of 2023, when more and more J-Pop artists started coming to Taiwan.

The first J-Pop show I saw live was Ryokuoushoku Shakai at Super Slippa. They even had an event where you could get something signed in person if you bought merchandise! I first got to know them through their cover of amazarashi's [空に歌えば](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=%E7%B7%91%E9%BB%84%E8%89%B2%E7%A4%BE%E4%BC%9A+%E7%A9%BA%E3%81%AB%E6%AD%8C%E3%81%88%E3%81%B0), and lately my favorite has been [花になって](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v-WcMQbXbKY). I love how grand and assertive it sounds, like it's proclaiming its own presence. By the way, it's also the theme song for _The Apothecary Diaries_.

But the artists who made me fall completely for J-Pop were Airi Suzuki and milet.

I first learned about Airi Suzuki through [DADDY! DADDY! DO!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Od7QCsyqkE), by Masayuki Suzuki feat. Airi Suzuki. It was also the theme song for season two of _Kaguya-sama: Love Is War_. At first, I just thought it had a really refreshing, upbeat rhythm and was fun to sing along to.

I became completely captivated by her after I came across a YouTube video of her sharing her dance practice. I couldn't believe how someone could sing and dance so well. I was totally drawn to her.

I was doing my military service in Kinmen at the time, and whenever I got to use my phone, I'd listen to Airi Suzuki nonstop. The background in the music video for [start again](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=%E9%88%B4%E6%9C%A8%E6%84%9B%E7%90%86+start+again) reminded me so much of Kinmen. Even now, whenever I hear the song, I can almost feel Kinmen's strong winds.

Airi Suzuki happened to hold a solo concert in Taipei this month. Seeing her bring her band and dancers all the way to Taiwan and dance up a storm was so moving.

I discovered milet through [Prover](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=milet+Prover+Official+Music+Video), the ending theme for _Fate/Grand Order: Absolute Demonic Front — Babylonia_. I thought it fit the anime's atmosphere perfectly. The legendary Gilgamesh is the last demigod, facing a beast invasion that threatens to destroy his kingdom. The song captures that vast, desolate feeling, with a touch of divinity, so perfectly in milet's voice.

When I first looked up the music video, I thought the person starring in it was beautiful. Only after searching did I realize she was the singer herself. The contrast between her appearance and her voice surprised me.

The first solo concert I ever attended was also milet's “Hey Taipei, I'm milet” at the end of 2023. Before then, I couldn't understand why anyone would spend so much money to go to a concert. After that show, I realized I was one of those people.

I also have to mention Ado. I got into her through _One Piece Film: Red_. I love [New Genesis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1FliVTcX8bQ), [私は最強](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ado+%E7%A7%81%E3%81%AF%E6%9C%80%E5%BC%B7), and [逆光](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ado+%E9%80%86%E5%85%89), and lately my favorite has been [Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pgXpM4l_MwI).

After seeing her concert, I came to love how she shares so many of her thoughts onstage. She's a world-class singer, yet the issue she keeps working through is self-love. I recently bought her autobiography, but I still haven't read it.

Finally, let me recommend a book: _Audubon's Prayer_, by Kotaro Isaka. You'll understand why I recommended it here once you've finished reading it.
